Opal

Making focus feel a little easier. Students don’t need another app telling them to focus. With two teammates, I designed a focus app around planning, motivation, and breaks worth taking, built from a 30-student survey and tested with 23 more.

Role
Product Designer
Team
3 designers
Timeline
10 weeks, 2025
Recognition
Indigo Design Award
Goal
Help students actually get focused work done, without another app just blocking things.
The idea
If a focus app combines planning, a reward for progress, and breaks that guide you back, students will finish more of what they planned, because 27 of 30 we surveyed said procrastination was the problem, not a lack of blocking.
How I’d know
Planned tasks finished per session, and how many breaks end with the student coming back instead of scrolling. If completion didn’t beat a plain blocker, I’d be wrong.
What changed my mind
Testing with 23 people showed “Event” didn’t make sense and new users got lost, so we switched to Task and Activity and added first-time help.

Goal

Students didn’t want stricter blocking.

We surveyed 30 college students. They weren’t asking for stricter blocking. They wanted help staying motivated, planning their time, and taking breaks without falling straight back into scrolling.

27/30
struggled with procrastination
21/30
were distracted by their phones
61%
had never used a focus app

The idea

If one app covered the whole cycle, students would stick with it.

  • ForestBlocks distractions.
  • FinchMakes self-care rewarding.
  • Focus FriendAdds accountability.

Each app solved one piece. Students wanted pieces of all three, so the bet was one app that did all of it.

The idea, built

One app for the whole cycle: plan, focus, step away.

How I’d know · What changed my mind

23 people showed us where we were wrong.

  • “Event” didn’t make senseWe switched to familiar words like Task and Activity, with icons.
  • Scanning needed guidanceWe added alignment guides and clearer onboarding for Tap Stickers.
  • New users got lostWe added help the first time someone meets a feature.

What I took away

Make the better choice the easier one.

The real question wasn’t how to stop distraction. It was how to make putting the phone down feel better than picking it back up.
