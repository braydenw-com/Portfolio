Slack · UX Research

Making Slack’s hidden state visible

As research lead on a team of three, I studied where everyday Slack tasks break down. Two of them failed for most people, and two small changes to language and system state took success from 3/8 and 2/8 to 8/8.

Overview

Slack has the features. People can’t find them.

We reviewed 20 Slack workflows against Nielsen’s heuristics, then watched 8 people try the everyday ones. Getting around was fine. Two tasks fell apart, and most people thought they’d finished.

Saved messagesMaking it obvious who a saved or pinned message is for. NotificationsShowing what Slack will alert you about, and why.
  • Hidden statePeople couldn’t tell what was saved, pinned, or switched on.
  • Look-alike labelsSaved, Pinned, and Later all sounded the same.
  • Scattered controlsNotification settings lived across several menus.

Saved messages

The concept wasn’t the problem. The language was.

Saved, Pinned, and Later all sounded alike, and none said who could see what. We renamed the actions around ownership: Save for Me, Pin for Everyone.

Notifications

People can’t change what they can’t see.

Notification controls were spread across menus, with no sign of what was already on. We added a persistent status and put every control in one place.

What I took away

The interface didn’t need more features. It needed to explain the ones it had.

We validated both changes with 8 new people on UserTesting, with no researcher in the room. The same failures didn’t come back.
