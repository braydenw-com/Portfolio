## intro title
Making a case study

## intro summary
**Nobody is digging through 43 Figma frames to understand what you built.** Make it easy for people to see what you made, how you got there, and what you contributed. A good case study covers four things: the overview, the problem, the decisions, and the results.

## overview label
01 · The Overview

## overview heading
Show the finished work first.

## overview paragraph
A strong hero image, a brief description of what you built, and the basics: your role, team, and timeline.

## overview do title
Example: Opal

## overview do text
The page opens on the three new features, then one line: “Making focus feel a little easier.” Role, team, and timeline sit right under it, before any process.

## overview skip title
Skip

## overview skip text
A project timeline or a mood board before anyone has seen what you made.

## problem label
02 · The Problem

## problem heading
Explain what you were trying to solve and why it mattered.

## problem paragraph
Give people just enough context to understand the project and why it was worth doing.

## problem do title
Example: Slack

## problem do text
“Two everyday tasks were quietly failing.” Then the proof in two numbers: only 3 of 8 people found a saved message, and 2 of 8 managed their notifications.

## problem skip title
Skip

## problem skip text
A 20-slide research summary. Keep the one finding that explains why the project exists.

## decisions label
03 · The Decisions

## decisions heading
Show why you chose one approach over another.

## decisions paragraph
Highlight the key insights, ideas you explored, and trade-offs that shaped your direction.

## decisions do title
Example: Slack and Opal

## decisions do text
Slack: fix the words before adding features. Opal: build on the existing app instead of designing one more focus app. Each says what was given up and why.

## decisions skip title
Skip

## decisions skip text
Every sticky note and every idea you tried. Show the fork in the road and the side you picked.

## results label
04 · The Results

## results heading
Put the finished work front and center.

## results paragraph
Show the final designs, explain the key features, and include feedback, testing, or outcomes that demonstrate what worked.

## results do title
Example: Slack

## results do text
3/8 → 8/8 with new people in unmoderated testing, next to the participant who said “There is usually a saved button here. This is terribly confusing.”

## results skip title
Skip

## results skip text
“Users loved it.” Show where people stumbled, what changed, and what happened next.

## takeaway label
The takeaway

## takeaway heading
Your portfolio is a product, too.

## takeaway paragraph
The hiring manager is your user. Apply your design knowledge to your own site.
