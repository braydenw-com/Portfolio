## intro title
Slack

## intro summary
**Making Slack’s hidden state visible.** As research lead on a team of three, I studied where everyday Slack tasks break down. Two of them failed for most people, and two small changes to language and system state took success from 3/8 and 2/8 to 8/8.

## opportunity label
The opportunity

## opportunity heading
Two everyday tasks were quietly failing.

## opportunity paragraph
We reviewed 20 Slack workflows against Nielsen’s heuristics, then watched 8 people try the everyday ones. Getting around was fine. Two tasks fell apart, and most people thought they’d finished.

## opportunity point 1 title

## opportunity point 1 text

## opportunity point 2 text

## did saved messages label
What I did · Saved messages

## did saved messages heading
The concept wasn’t the problem. The language was.

## did saved messages paragraph
Saved, Pinned, and Later all sounded alike, and none said who could see what. We renamed the actions around ownership: Save for Me, Pin for Everyone.

“There is usually a saved button here. This is terribly confusing.”

## did notifications heading
Showing people what Slack will notify them about.

## did notifications paragraph
Notification controls were spread across menus, with no sign of what was already on. We added a persistent status and put every control in one place.

## result label
The result

## result heading
Most of the fix was wording and visibility, not new features.

## result paragraph
We validated both changes with 8 new people on UserTesting, with no researcher in the room. The same failures didn’t come back.

## learned label
What I learned

## learned heading
Watch people before deciding what to fix.

## learned paragraph
Our heuristic review told us where to look. Only watching people get stuck showed us why, and every change we made came from something we saw in a session.
